How to compare compact Bitcoin ASIC miners

Compact miners (Bitaxe-class devices, USB stick miners and small desktop units) are compared with the same three numbers as their industrial cousins — just at a smaller scale. This page is a short checklist you can apply to any model.

1. Efficiency: joules per terahash (J/TH)

Efficiency tells you how much energy the chip needs per unit of work. Lower is better. A device twice as efficient costs half as much in electricity for the same hashrate. Note whether the figure is for the chip or the whole device including its power supply; wall efficiency is the number your socket sees.

2. Power draw in watts

Hashrate alone says little. Multiply watts by 24 by your kWh price for a daily electricity cost. A quiet-sounding 15 W device and a 200 W device differ by an order of magnitude in running cost, even if both are “compact”.

3. Noise and heat

Manufacturer decibel figures are usually measured at one metre in ideal conditions. Fanless and passive-cooled designs trade hashrate for silence; expect a small miner to still add a meaningful heat load to a room at 100 W+.

4. Total cost of ownership

Compare: device cost + electricity over your holding period − mined sats at realistic (not peak) difficulty. Difficulty rises over time, so a margin that looks thin today usually gets thinner.
